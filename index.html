Disasters and site failures by year

Disasters are events that harmed people, land or the river beyond the project site. Site failures stayed within the site but show unstable ground or weak construction. Worker accidents are not included.

Flood from dam release Flood the dam failed to control Landslide or failure at project Site failure (warning sign)

For many incidents there is no official count of people affected, houses lost, livestock killed or crops damaged, and for some even deaths are uncertain. Project developers rarely publish incident reports, and district flood figures often don't separate a dam release from rain. The real scale of many incidents is likely larger than shown here, so treat these numbers as a minimum.

What this does and doesn't cover

  • The land total is a floor, not a full count. No official source adds up land under all existing dams; operating projects like Panyor Lower and Pare have no published land figures I could find.
  • Project counts, charts and tables cover large hydro only (over 25 MW), the line the Central Electricity Authority uses. Small hydro is shown as one figure: the Ministry of Power's installed capacity as of 31 Dec 2024. No official list or count of the small plants was found, so the number of plants, their land use and any incidents at them are not covered. Dikshi (24 MW) and Khangtang (7 MW), which the state lists as commissioned, are among them.
  • The state's own list of commissioned projects (5, Mar 2026) differs from the "Projects existing" count because it includes those two small plants and leaves out Subansiri Lower, which sits on the Assam border.
  • Flood counts for 2017 and 2021 are district-wide, so they include damage from rain as well as from the dam. They are kept because a dam that releases or fails to hold back water can make a flood worse.
  • "Forest land taken" counts only Arunachal's share; Subansiri Lower diverted another 816 ha in Assam.
  • The state counts 33 projects coming up; the table lists only those with named public records.
  • Worker accidents at construction sites are left out: this dashboard tracks harm to people, land and rivers beyond the projects.
  • The disaster and site-failure lists rely partly on SANDRP, an advocacy group that tracks dam accidents; each entry points back to the news report it cites. Loss of life, where mentioned, is as reported by local media, not an official toll.
  • Dam heights for Dibang (278 m, cut from 288 m) and Siang Upper (~300 m) are as reported; Siang Upper is still at survey stage.
